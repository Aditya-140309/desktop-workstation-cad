Introduction & CAD/CAM Overview

Project introduction

A desktop workstation is a familiar assembly made of several distinct parts that must fit and work together on a desk. This showcase treats it as a design-communication exercise: breaking the assembly into components and describing it through standard orthographic views.

CAD/CAM design overview

CAD (computer-aided design) is about representing a product: its shape, parts and drawings. CAM (computer-aided manufacturing) is about turning a design into a way of making it. This page focuses on the design and documentation side and explains how it connects to manufacturing in general terms.

Design Process

A typical CAD/CAM workflow for an assembly like this, shown for explanation. It is not a record of specific software or steps used.

  1. Study referencesReview reference drawings and photos of the workstation.
  2. Break down the assemblyIdentify cabinet, monitor, keyboard, mouse and stand.
  3. Orthographic viewsDescribe the product through top, front and side views.
  4. 3D modellingBuild each part as a solid, then position it in an assembly.
  5. Check fit & layoutConfirm parts sit together sensibly on the desk.
  6. DocumentPrepare drawings and notes for communication.
  7. CAM preparationIf a part were to be made, plan the process, material and toolpaths.

Learning Outcomes

  • Reading and relating top, front and side views of one object.
  • Decomposing an assembly into well-defined components.
  • Understanding how CAD representations connect to CAM planning.
  • Communicating a design clearly with drawings and an interactive model.
  • Separating reference material from original work in documentation.
